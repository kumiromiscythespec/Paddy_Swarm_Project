# Safety and Emergency Overflow

Every local sump has an independent HIGH outlet, minimum 25 mm and preferred
32 mm internal flow diameter. The route is local sump -> normally dry common
safety trough -> non-circulating emergency receiver.

It must not return to the equalization trunk or common pump well. This keeps an
isolated or disease-suspect sump isolated, exposes abnormal flow visibly and
prevents fault discharge from becoming normal circulation.

The final receiver volume is not selected. It is sized from branch flow,
detection/response time, simultaneous fault count and safety margin. At the
0.5-1.0 L/min branch target, one unnoticed branch contributes 2.5-5 L in five
minutes, 5-10 L in ten minutes and 7.5-15 L in fifteen minutes.

Other controls:

- Keep every sump atmospheric or vented under a light-blocking cover.
- Keep the bypass available so all eight supply valves cannot dead-head pump.
- Independently support hoses, trunk, valves and electrical cable.
- Protect the rear service path and low-point drain from impact.
- Put electrical connectors above possible HIGH/overflow levels with drip
  loops and appropriate supply-side protection.
- Never lift the pump by its cable.
- Treat wet floors, unexpected dry-trough flow and LOW-level alarms as stop
  conditions.
